Scientific Foundation & Problem Statement

🎯 Research Framework: Aims, Objectives & Working Hypotheses

Investigating the biophysical trade-offs of fen peatland rewetting: decoupling the aerobic-to-anaerobic transition across soil mechanics, micrometeorology, and radiative forcing horizons.

GENERAL OBJECTIVE

Quantify continuous high-frequency greenhouse gas fluxes (CO₂, CH₄, N₂O) and determine how decades of agricultural soil compaction (precompression stress & plow pan formation) modulate biogeochemical gas exchange, ebullition dynamics, and net radiative forcing during the drained-to-rewetted transition at Wallener Au.

HYPOTHESIS 1 Biogeochemical

The Biogeochemical Pulse & Acute CH₄ Surge

Elevating the groundwater table above the critical -15 cm threshold suppresses aerobic heterotrophic respiration (reducing CO₂ by >70%), but the immediate immersion of labile agricultural root biomass and residual fertilizer triggers an acute, non-linear surge of CH₄ emissions that exerts potent front-loaded radiative forcing.

Hydrological Trigger: WTD > -15 cm
Redox Tipping Point: Eh < -200 mV
Climate Sensitivity: GWP₂₀ = 84 Dominance
HYPOTHESIS 2 Soil Mechanics

Soil Mechanical Memory & The Hydrological Paradox

Decades of heavy tractor axle loads created an over-consolidated plow pan (12–35 cm, σp = 62.4 kPa) that acts as a dual physical bottleneck: breaking capillary rise during summer droughts (vegetation desiccation) and trapping infiltration during wet periods into perched anoxic pools that force localized methanogenesis.

Precompression Stress: σp = 62.4 ± 4.8 kPa
Hydraulic Collapse: Ks: 10⁻⁴ ➔ 10⁻⁷ m/s
Macropore Loss: -85% (>50 µm pores)
HYPOTHESIS 3 Spatial Scaling

Spatial Disconnect: Hotspot Dilution

Relict agricultural ditches and microtopographical swales act as localized ebullition chimneys whose extreme point fluxes (+300% CH₄ vs tower average) are aerodynamically diluted when integrated across the 200 m eddy covariance footprint, requiring coupled multiscale scaling.

Chamber Ditch Bias: +300% CH₄ vs Tower
Aerodynamic Fetch: 120–250 m (Kljun 2015)
Scaling Solution: Chamber-Tower Bridge

⚗️ Biogeochemistry & Redox Dynamics

Microbial cascading pathways, terminal electron acceptors, and gas exchange stoichiometry governing carbon transformations across oxic-anoxic peat profiles.

Stoichiometry

Gas Exchange Redox Equations

● Oxic Mineralization (Aerobic Respiration) Drained Active
C_org + O₂ ➔ CO₂ + H₂O   (ΔG°' = -2870 kJ/mol)
Dominant in drained acrotelm. Oxygen acts as highest-energy terminal electron acceptor, driving rapid peat oxidation.
● Incomplete Denitrification (Fluctuating / Suboxic)
NO₃⁻ ➔ NO₂⁻ ➔ NO ➔ N₂O ↑   (➔ N₂)
Fluctuating water tables trigger high-GWP N₂O pulses (GWP₁₀₀ = 273) when trace oxygen inhibits nitrous oxide reductase.
● Methanogenesis (Strict Anoxia, Eh < -200 mV) Rewetted Spike
Hydrogenotrophic: CO₂ + 4H₂ ➔ CH₄ + 2H₂O
Acetoclastic: CH₃COOH ➔ CH₄ + CO₂
Terminal step of anaerobic degradation executed by Archaea after alternative electron acceptors are fully depleted.
● Methanotrophy (Biological Methane Filter) Biofilter Active
CH₄ + 2O₂ ➔ CO₂ + 2H₂O   (ΔG°' = -820 kJ/mol)
Aerobic methanotrophic bacteria consume 60–90% of upward diffusing CH₄ at the capillary fringe before reaching atmosphere.
Succession Dynamics

Ecological Succession & Community Shift

DRAINED AGRICULTURAL BASELINE

Oxic Degradation & Aerobic Dominance

  • Key Drivers: Deep water table (< -40 cm), persistent oxic acrotelm, rapid gas diffusion into peat matrix.
  • Microbial Consortia: Aerobic bacteria, saprophytic fungi, actinobacteria activate extracellular phenol oxidases.
  • Carbon Fate: Accelerated peat oxidation, severe subsidence (1–2 cm/yr), sustained net source (15–25 t CO₂-eq ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹).
  • Biofilter Status: Quiescent; negligible anaerobic CH₄ substrate supply.
REWETTED RESTORATION TRAJECTORY

Thermodynamic Inversion & Trophic Succession

  • Phase 1 (Transient 0–2 yr): Acute Methane Spike! Rapid O₂ exhaustion triggers severe redox collapse (Eh < -200 mV); fermenters and methanogenic archaea metabolize labile dead root biomass.
  • Phase 2 (Equilibrium >3 yr): Sphagnum/Carex recolonization establishes a stratified microbial biofilter. Methanotrophic bacteria in top 5 cm consume 60–90% of CH₄.
  • Bypass Conduit: Vascular plant aerenchymas act as direct chimneys, bypassing surface oxidation.
  • Net Climate Impact: Long-term CO₂ respiration halted by >85%; restored peat accumulation.
Integrative Conceptual Framework

Peatlands Biogeochemical Processes (Fig. 20)

Wetland GHG Cascade & Redox Hierarchy

Unified biogeochemical continuum: photosynthesis fixes atmospheric carbon; aerobic respiration in the surface acrotelm generates CO₂; under waterlogged anoxic conditions, sequential reduction of nitrate, ferric iron, and sulfate proceeds until methanogenesis is triggered. Aerobic methanotrophic bacteria in the unsaturated surface zone oxidize 60–90% of diffusing CH₄, except where bypassed by vascular plant aerenchyma conduits or episodic bubble ebullition.

Microbial Consortia

Key Biogeochemical Actors

DEPOLYMERIZATION AEROBIC (FUNGI/BACTERIA)
Extracellular Enzymes
Secreted by fungi and aerobic bacteria to depolymerize structural peat compounds (cellulose, lignin, phenolics). Controlled by the "phenol oxidase latch".
Substrate: Structural Peat Polymers
ACIDOGENESIS ANAEROBIC BACTERIA
Fermentative Bacteria
Anaerobically break down soluble monosaccharides and amino acids into short-chain fatty acids (acetate, propionate, butyrate), H₂, and CO₂.
Substrate: Soluble Monomers
ACETOGENESIS SYNTROPHIC ANAEROBES
Acetogenic Microorganisms
Synthesize acetate from H₂ and CO₂ via the Wood-Ljungdahl pathway or oxidize volatile acids, providing critical direct precursor for methanogenesis.
Substrate: H₂ + CO₂ & Volatile Fatty Acids
METHANOGENESIS STRICT ANAEROBIC ARCHAEA
Methanogenic Archaea
Strictly anaerobic Archaea producing CH₄ via acetoclastic cleavage or CO₂ reduction with H₂ in permanently waterlogged peat horizons (Eh < -200 mV).
Substrate: Acetate & H₂/CO₂
OXIDATION CAPILLARY BIOFILTER
Methanotrophic Bacteria
Obligate aerobic methane oxidizers residing at the oxic-anoxic boundary that convert up to 90% of CH₄ into CO₂ and biomass before release.
Substrate: CH₄ + O₂
VASCULAR CONDUIT BYPASS CHIMNEY
Plants with Aerenchyma
Wetland vegetation (*Carex*, *Eriophorum*, *Typha*) providing internal gas channels that vent deep CH₄ straight to the atmosphere, bypassing the biofilter.
Substrate: Root Exudates & Gas Transport

Computational Workflow & Machine Learning Architecture

💻 Computational Architecture: From Raw 10Hz to Ecosystem Budgets

From raw 10 Hz binary files to definitive GHG budgets — the 4-Sphere R computational architecture processes 14,993 half-hourly timesteps through EddyPro® 7.0.9 (3D planar fit, WPL corrections, ICOS 0-1-2 screening), REddyProc gap-filling (MDS + Lloyd-Taylor + Michaelis-Menten), and non-linear AI imputation (ANN & Random Forest) for episodic winter CH₄ ebullition gaps. Figs. 50, 52, 62 & 63 document the complete workflow architecture.

Phase 1 • High-Frequency

1. EC Processing & Quality Control (EddyPro & REddyProc)

14,000+ Raw 10 Hz Files
Core Pipeline Steps: Despiking → Planar Fit → WPL → Spectral Filter
Quality Screening: ICOS 0-1-2 Flags • Friction velocity u* > 0.12 m/s

Over 14,000 raw 10 Hz files were processed in 30-minute blocks. Spectral loss corrections and WPL density compensation prevent sensor heating and temperature fluctuations from propagating artifacts into turbulent fluxes.

Phase 2 • AI & Imputation

2. Gap-Filling & Machine Learning (ANN & Random Forest)

Non-Linear Methane Imputation
Model Architecture: Artificial Neural Network (ANN) & Random Forest Regressors
Predictive Drivers: Tsoil (5, 20, 50 cm), WTD, Net Radiation (Rn), u*

Winter optical window icing in open-path sensors creates critical data gaps. While CO₂ is imputed with standard Marginal Distribution Sampling (MDS), episodic CH₄ ebullition requires non-linear Machine Learning to capture hydrostatic pressure coupling and avoid underestimating annual emissions.

Phase 3 • Ecophysiology

3. Flux Partitioning: Decoupling NEE into GPP and Reco

Lloyd-Taylor & Michaelis-Menten
Respiration Model: Lloyd & Taylor (1994) Arrhenius Function (Reco)
Light-Response Model: Michaelis-Menten Hyperbola (GPP)
Mass Balance: NEE = Reco - GPP

Nocturnal NEE under well-developed turbulence (u* > 0.12 m/s) parameterizes the temperature sensitivity of microbial ecosystem respiration. Projecting Reco into the daytime separates photosynthetic carbon assimilation (GPP) from soil respiration.
